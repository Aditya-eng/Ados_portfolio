About

A bit about me…

“I read my way down from computers to CPUs to transistors, and realised that people had made rocks think by arranging them in the right order. I still find that remarkable.”

It started in ninth grade, in a robotics programme with my first Python classes. Getting a machine to follow code I had written was the most fun I'd had at school. That made me curious about what sits underneath the code. Nand2Tetris gave the curiosity a structure, taking me from gates up to a computer that runs programs. Along the way I wrote my own assembler for it in C++.

The layer I enjoy most turned out to be custom hardware on FPGAs. A CPU is a fixed arrangement of rocks. An FPGA lets me re-arrange them myself: define the hardware, load it, measure it, change it, and reload it. It's the closest thing to designing a chip without a fab.

At NSUT I went from Arduino, ESP32 and Raspberry Pi in the CEDT lab, to bringing up custom boards at the Texas Instruments Innovation Lab, to RTL, processor design and arithmetic architecture. I'm graduating in May 2027, and I want to become an FPGA / RTL design engineer by way of research-oriented graduate study in computer architecture.

“I don't trust a board until I've measured it working.”

Research

Research

B.Tech Project Supervisor: Dr. Jyoti Yadav · NSUT · Team of four, design lead

Signed Radix-4 Modified Russian Peasant Multiplier for Area-Efficient FIR Filters

In my B.Tech project I led the design within a four-member team. We extended a 2025 Modified Russian Peasant Multiplier into a signed, radix-4, Booth-recoded form. That replaces 8 sequential shift-add iterations with 4 parallel partial products. Using coefficient symmetry, we then folded an 8-tap linear-phase FIR onto 4 multipliers. I implemented it on a Xilinx Virtex-7 VC709 for signal denoising.

  • −35.7%LUT4 (345 → 222), bit-identical output
  • +55%Fmax with 4-stage pipeline (42.96 → 66.47 MHz)
  • 0mismatches across all 65,536 signed operand pairs

Questions I keep coming back to

01

Arithmetic vs. the system

When does a cleverer multiplier still help once registers, data movement, control overhead and power are counted?

02

FPGA accelerators for quantised NNs

Start with a conventional MAC datapath, compare alternative arithmetic at the same precision, then measure against a software reference.

03

Sparsity-aware compute

Does skipping zeros save enough to pay for the extra control and buffering?

04

Near-sensor processing

How much logic can sit beside a sensor before routing and logic costs outweigh the benefit?

05

HW/SW co-design

Accelerator integration: transfer and control overhead measured honestly against the software baseline.

06

Beyond FPGA metrics

Power and area in an ASIC flow, and energy at the system level, not just LUT counts.

On verification: I keep functional evidence separate from implementation guarantees. For example, my FIFO simulations show correct ordering, but they don't prove physical immunity to metastability.
